Methodology — v0.4

How we measure a megawatt.

Every figure Joule publishes comes from public market data and a reproducible model. This page states what we measure and how every figure can be checked — so your team can verify our numbers before you ever talk to us.

Data window: Nov 2025 – Oct 2026 · Sources: ENTSO-E Transparency Platform / SEMOpx / EPEX via energy-charts / SMARD / regelleistung.net / SEM Committee · Status: living document — DE re-verified 10 Nov 2026 against SMARD (max deviation €0.005/MWh); FR cross-validated 11 Nov 2026 against ENTSO-E official publication (100% point-identical on complete hours)

M—02Data sources

  • Day-ahead spot prices — EPEX (DE-LU, FR) via the public energy-charts.info API; SEMOpx for the Irish SEM; cross-validated against the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform (IE: 99.76% point-identical; FR: 100.00% point-identical across 7,742 complete-publication hours) and SMARD, the German federal regulator's platform (DE: max deviation €0.005/MWh).
  • Ancillary capacity prices — regelleistung.net (German TSO joint platform), twelve months of weekly tenders, FCR and aFRR.
  • Capacity market parameters — SEM Committee auction information packs and decision papers (CRM T-4 2028/29, 2029/30).

M—03Dispatch model

Three scheduling strategies compete on identical data:

oracle:    annual linear program over a 72 MWh (24 h) deferrable queue — the theoretical upper bound, computed with full hindsight
scheduler: Joule's production design — a rolling daily plan that uses only information published before each delivery day, frozen when day-ahead prices publish. No future information.
naive:     fixed night window 22:00–10:00 at 6 MW — the heuristic operators actually use

Capture rate = realized savings ÷ oracle savings, against a flat-schedule baseline. All accounting is energy-conserving at uniform hourly resolution.

The naive baseline is computed on UTC hours, matching our console's published configuration.

The naive line is not a strawman — it is what a cron job does, and it has quietly become a cost center. Its figure is window-fragile by construction: in Germany it still captured 44–46% in the wind season (Nov–Jan) but lost money in six of the twelve months, down to −23% in April; France swings from +38% in January to −27% in April; even in Ireland, the night window's natural habitat, it peaked at +61% in January yet dipped below zero in April and July. Solar has inverted the daily price shape the fixed window was built on.

We publish the uncomfortable corollary too: a price-aware day-ahead baseline already captures most of the oracle. Arbitrage alone is not a moat — anyone with published prices can do it. Joule's margin lives where day-ahead scheduling cannot go: cross-day banking, ancillary and capacity stacking, sub-minute response, and settlement-grade evidence.

Ancillary and capacity layers — FCR at a measured €141.9k/MW/yr, aFRR capacity adding a measured +€297k per 10 MW site per year (+29.9% on top of day-ahead arbitrage, oracle-grade ceiling, capacity revenue only, tenders covered 365/365 days), Irish CRM clearing at €135.5k/MW/yr — are inaccessible to any fixed-schedule strategy. Naive capture there is not low. It is zero.

Stacking the dispatchable share of these layers on top of arbitrage brings the total annual value pool for the reference site to ≈ €1.0–1.4M, depending on market and registered flexible capacity (3.6 MW of 10 MW in this configuration). Germany's measured stack — €992k arbitrage ceiling plus €297k aFRR capacity — alone reaches €1.29M at oracle grade. Site-specific figures are computed, not extrapolated, in every audit.

M—05Limitations

  • Oracle is an upper bound. Our production scheduler is gated at ≥60% capture; the measured figures above cleared that gate in all three markets, with only day-ahead information.
  • Hourly resolution understates intraday optionality; telemetry-grade modeling ships with audits.
  • Capture rate is conditional on site load-flexibility parameters. Site values are recalibrated on metered load at pilot.
  • The aFRR layer is an oracle-grade ceiling: capacity revenue only, activation energy excluded, and market prequalification for load-side participation not yet modelled.
  • All three markets are now dual-source verified; the window closes Oct 2026 and rolls forward in our console as new official data publishes.
  • No storage assumed. Storage-backed configurations unlock FCR symmetry and are assessed per site.
