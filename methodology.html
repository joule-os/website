Methodology — v0.7

How we measure a megawatt.

Every figure Joule publishes comes from public market data and a reproducible model. This page states what we measure and how every figure can be checked — so your team can verify our numbers before you ever talk to us.

Training energy is conserved: 72 MWh per day arrive and must be served; the queue may carry at most 24 h of arrivals (72 MWh), so work can cross midnight but never accumulates beyond a day — flexibility means when, never whether. All dispatch figures are computed on this IT-side flexible-load energy; meter-side settlement scales with the site's metered PUE and is recalibrated at pilot. Cooling and base load sit outside the dispatch model and are not scaled twice.

M—02Data sources

  • Day-ahead spot prices — EPEX (DE-LU, FR) via the public energy-charts.info API; SEMOpx for the Irish SEM (Nov 2025 – May 2026, native 30-minute), spliced with ENTSO-E's official 60-minute IE_SEM publication (Jun – Oct 2026; the May 2026 overlap is 100% point-identical across 742 hours); cross-validated against the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform (IE: 99.76% point-identical; FR: 100.00% point-identical across the 7,742 complete-publication hours of 8,760 — excluded hours lack complete official 15-minute blocks; exclusion register on request) and SMARD, the German federal regulator's platform (DE: max deviation €0.005/MWh).
  • Ancillary capacity prices — regelleistung.net (German TSO joint platform), twelve months of daily tenders (4-hour capacity products), FCR and aFRR.
  • Capacity market parameters — SEM Committee auction information packs and decision papers (CRM T-4 2028/29, 2029/30).

M—03Dispatch model

Three scheduling strategies compete on identical data:

oracle:    annual linear program over a 72 MWh (24 h) deferrable queue — the upper bound of this model, computed with full hindsight
scheduler: Joule's production design — a rolling daily plan that uses only information published before each delivery day, frozen when day-ahead prices publish. No future information.
naive:     fixed night window 22:00–10:00 at the model's 6 MW catch-up peak — the heuristic operators actually use (see E-03)

Capture rate = realized savings ÷ oracle savings. The flat-schedule baseline runs the training load at its constant average arrival rate (3 MW, 24/7). The scheduler plans each local delivery day and freezes its plan when day-ahead prices publish, so it knows every price of the day it plans; the residual gap to the oracle arises at day boundaries, where backlog carried past local midnight is valued at a forecast-based terminal price (7-day persistence) instead of actual future prices. All accounting is energy-conserving at the market's native time resolution — 15-minute for Germany and France, 60-minute for Ireland as officially published (see M—05).

The naive line is not a strawman — it is what a cron job does, and it has quietly become a cost center. Its figure is window-fragile by construction: in Germany it still captured 45–49% in the wind season (Nov–Jan) but lost money in seven of the twelve months, down to −55% in July; France swings from +40% in January to −53% in July and is negative in eight months of twelve; even in Ireland, the night window's natural habitat, it peaked at +61% in January yet dipped below zero in five months, worst −26% in July. Solar has inverted the daily price shape the fixed window was built on.

We publish the uncomfortable corollary too: a price-aware day-ahead baseline already captures most of the oracle. Arbitrage alone is not a moat — anyone with published prices can do it. Joule's margin lives where fixed day-ahead schedules cannot go: queue banking across midnight (and beyond 24 h where workloads allow), ancillary and capacity stacking, sub-minute response, and settlement-grade evidence.

Ancillary and capacity layers — FCR capacity priced at €141.9k/MW/yr (market price observation; symmetric response from load requires storage backing — excluded from the no-storage value pool below), an aFRR capacity ceiling of +€297k per 10 MW site per year (≈+29% of the day-ahead arbitrage ceiling; joint-LP result at hourly resolution, capacity revenue only, activation energy excluded, tenders covered 365/365 days), Irish CRM T-4 2029/30 clearing at €135.5k/MW/yr (before de-rating and availability obligations) — are inaccessible to any fixed-schedule strategy. Naive capture there is not low. It is zero.

These layers compete for the same flexible megawatts, so the attainable stack is not an arithmetic sum. Measured jointly on the German window (oracle-grade co-optimization of arbitrage and aFRR capacity, hourly resolution — native-resolution re-run queued), the arbitrage layer yields €111k of its standalone hourly ceiling to hold reserve: €881k arbitrage + €297k aFRR = €1.18M joint stack. Summing the standalone hourly ceilings without interaction would read €1.29M — a number we do not bank. Across markets, the pre-interaction value pool computes to ≈ €1.0–1.4M per year, depending on market and registered flexible capacity (reserve offers are sized block-by-block against the deferrable queue — sustained means of 2.2–2.4 MW in the German run; an earlier "3.6 MW" figure had no engine derivation and is corrected, see E-07 / E-11). Site-specific figures are computed, not extrapolated, in every audit.

M—05Limitations

  • Oracle is an upper bound. Our production scheduler is gated at ≥60% capture — an internal acceptance threshold, not an external benchmark; the measured figures above cleared that gate in all three markets, with only day-ahead information. The dispatch model covers the training queue; tier-2 inference flexibility (0.25 MW, minutes-scale) is outside the energy-arbitrage layer.
  • Market time units: since 1 Oct 2025 the coupled day-ahead market (SDAC) clears in 15-minute products (Ireland's SEM: 30-minute). The M—04 figures are computed at native granularity for Germany and France (15-minute series as published). Ireland is modelled at 60-minute resolution — the granularity of ENTSO-E's official IE_SEM publication; a native 30-minute partial-window check (Nov 2025 – May 2026) shows a +0.6% oracle and +0.7pp capture effect, and the full-window 30-minute re-run is queued pending SEMOpx historical data access. The ancillary-stack figures quoted in M—04 remain hourly-resolution joint results; their native-resolution re-run is queued. Telemetry-grade modeling ships with audits.
  • Capture rate is conditional on site load-flexibility parameters. Site values are recalibrated on metered load at pilot.
  • The aFRR layer is an oracle-grade ceiling: capacity revenue only, activation energy excluded (it may add to or subtract from the ceiling), and market prequalification for load-side participation not yet modelled.
  • All three price series are cross-checked against a second publication of the same primary auction data (SMARD and ENTSO-E republish the EPEX/SEMOpx outcomes) — this validates our ingestion pipeline, not the auction source itself. The window closes Oct 2026 and rolls forward in our console as new official data publishes.
  • No storage assumed. The FCR price quoted in M—04 is a market observation requiring symmetric response; without storage the sustainable symmetric envelope is limited, so FCR revenue is excluded from the reference-site value pool. Storage-backed configurations are assessed per site.
